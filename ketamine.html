Figure excerpt from master's thesis showing quantification of normalized FUNCAT signal in whole hippocampus.

Mean pixel intensity was measured from images of hippocampal samples taken using a confocal microscope at 20x to estimate average FUNCAT signal relative to saline control. A significant increase in FUNCAT signal was found in the ketamine treated animals compared to saline controls.

About the Project

Our work attempts to address a central question in the study of the effects of ketamine: are the effects of ketamine due to changes in inhibitory neurons. Additionally, we set the groundwork to ask the following question: how are the electrophysiological and molecular effects related, that is, what is the relationship between the effects of ketamine across different levels of biology?

During my time at the labs of Dr. André Fenton and Dr. Eric Klann at the NYU Center for Neural Science, I used in vivo metabolic labeling (FUNCAT) to fluorescently tag nascent proteins after ketamine treatment in mice. Used confocal microscope to image hippocampus samples and standard image processing (ImageJ) and statistical methods (Prism) for data analysis.

I performed both single unit and population level analysis on data from neuropixel recordings in the mouse hippocampus during ketamine treatment, wrote custom notebooks (Python) and managed high performance computing environments for analysis of large datasets (neural recordings and proteomics).
